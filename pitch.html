racinglines · Pitch · 30 September 2026

An edge in race-sport prediction markets,
from Formula 1 to the downhill World Cup.

We turn official race timing into fair odds, priced strictly as of each session, and build the tools that help traders find and size an edge inside the existing markets: F1 on Polymarket and Kalshi first, now NASCAR and MotoGP. Downhill is priced and waiting for a market.

What we do

Tools for makers and takers on Polymarket and Kalshi: calls with heat ratings, quotes, alerts, an Edge Finder, all driven by as-of race models tested on every real price and trade.

Where we are

F1: two strategies that held in a held-out season on Polymarket; Kalshi paper signals wired. NASCAR and MotoGP are in the app as labelled demos. Next: live paper at round 16 (2–4 Oct) and an invite-only fantasy trading soft launch from Singapore (8 Oct).

How we earn

The tools, for traders who want an edge; B2B sales of the prediction models and pricing; and trading ourselves once paper trading validates the backtests.

The rule

A new sport qualifies when its audience and expected market liquidity sit between the two endpoints. Nothing smaller than the downhill World Cup; F1 is the ceiling we already trade against.

The test for each candidate

Official timing data we can get, exchange listings (or a clear path to them), and a model that fits the race format.

Why both ends

F1 proves the trading edge on a deep market. Downhill proves the model on a sport nobody else prices, ready the day a market lists it, and sellable B2B before then.

The edge

Markets move after every session. Our tools move with them, on what's known.

Priced as of each session

Only sessions that have ended feed the price; a leakage guard enforces it. Backtests, the signal engine and the live forecast call the same function.

Tested on the real tape

Every Polymarket F1 race market since the markets began (2025–26): minute prices and every trade. Maker fills only when a real trade crosses our quote.

The goal

Not to out-predict the whole market, which on race winners is about as sharp as the model. It's to find the few markets where the price is off, size them sensibly, and quote where it's safe: head-to-head markets made money in both seasons.

Schemas, not code

A new exchange or a tape-only sport is one schema file. OG.com was added that way; NASCAR and MotoGP added a results adapter, a market-link pass and a model on top.

Nothing trades real money: both venues' trading switches are off and OG.com has no order code. "In-sample" means the settings were picked on the same races they are scored on.

Reading the demo numbers

The NASCAR Pro demo record on Kalshi is +$6,525 for 2026 but −$278 for 2025, and its settings were picked on those same seasons. MotoGP's is +$31 on 11 races. They show the pipeline working end to end, not an edge.

What would change the verdict

Kalshi bid/ask prices instead of last trades; a naive baseline (market-implied, Plackett–Luce) to beat; a second season of markets; MotoGP sprints and calendar. NASCAR for 2027 is decided in December on this evidence.

Two strategies

A takes the calls. C makes the market.

A · core taker

Re-prices every race market after each practice and qualifying session and buys only when the model and the market disagree by 10 points or more (5 on head-to-head), sized by the gap, at most $50 a market. With the head-to-head threshold: +$1,243 (2026) and +$1,363 (2025).

C · maker sleeve

Quotes 2¢ either side of the gbm model's fair price, 25 shares a side, leaning against its inventory, and steps out of any market where the model and the market disagree by more than 5 points. Smallest drawdowns of any setup: $279 / $176.

The Lab's Edge Finder (2026): the setups the demo maker tested. In 2026 alone the grid-aware maker (M2) did better than C; C was chosen on 2025's evidence as the steadiest, and its 2026 result is within the noise.
