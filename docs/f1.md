# F1 model

F1 is the second sport, and the first one with liquid markets. It uses the same
database, web app and private book as downhill, and its own model family
(`racinglines/models/position_sim/`), which
uses **track and sector properties** in both training and prediction.

## Data source

**The official Formula 1 live-timing archive**, read with
[FastF1](https://docs.fastf1.dev) (the Python client for it). For every
qualifying, sprint and race session since 2018 it has:

- **Classification:** driver (Ergast-compatible `driverId`), team, grid, finishing
  position, classified status, official points, Q1/Q2/Q3 times.
- **Every lap:** lap and sector times, four speed traps (sector 1, sector 2, finish
  line, main straight), tyre compound and age, stint, pit in/out, track status
  (green / yellow / safety car / VSC / red), position, and whether FastF1 rates the
  timing as accurate.
- **Weather** through the session: air and track temperature, rain, humidity, wind.

No other single source has lap- and sector-level timing going back to 2020.
FastF1 limits itself to **500 API calls per hour** (about 65 sessions), so a full
2020–2026 backfill takes several hours. The fetcher waits out the limit rather
than failing, and fetches the newest seasons first.

```
racinglines f1 fetch                       # newest first: 2026, 2025, …, 2020; skips sessions already on disk
racinglines f1 fetch --years 2026          # e.g. after a race weekend
racinglines f1 ingest --years 2020-2026    # into PostgreSQL (idempotent)
```

Raw files go to `data/raw/f1/fastf1/<year>/<round>_<session>.{results,laps}.parquet` plus a
`.meta.json` (event, circuit, date, format, weather summary). FastF1's own HTTP
cache lives in `data/cache/fastf1`. Sprints are fetched only from `--sprints-from`
(default 2026), because the model trains on qualifying and races and only needs
the current season's sprints for championship points.

### Track profile (per event)

These come from each driver's fastest clean qualifying lap (median across the
field) and from the race classification:

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `lap_s` | Typical qualifying lap time |
| `s1_share`, `s2_share`, `s3_share` | Share of the lap spent in each sector |
| `v_i1`, `v_i2`, `v_fl`, `v_st` | Median speed-trap speeds: sector 1, sector 2, finish line, main straight (km/h) |
| `speed_index` | Time-weighted trap speed: Σ sector share × that sector's trap speed |
| `mean_places_gained` | Mean abs(grid − finish) of classified drivers, a proxy for how easy overtaking is |
| `grid_finish_rank_corr` | Rank correlation between grid and finish |
| `street` | Street circuit (`registry.STREET_CIRCUITS`) |
| `qual_rain_share`, `race_rain_share`, `*_track_temp` | Weather |

## Model (`racinglines/models/position_sim/model.py`)

Everything is built in date order, so each race's features use only data from
before it.

1. **Measurements per past event**
   - **Qualifying:** each driver's best lap as a % deficit to pole, and each
     team's best time in each sector as a % deficit to the fastest.
   - **Race pace:** each driver's median clean race lap as a % deficit. Clean
     means green flag, accurate timing, no pit in/out laps, not lap 1, within 107%
     of the field median, and at least 8 such laps.
2. **Sector-type car model (the track/sector part)**
   - For each team, fit `sector deficit = a + b·x`, where `x = (sector trap speed −
     250) / 50`, over its recent qualifying sectors.
   - The fit is weighted by recency (half-life 120 days) and ridge-shrunk:
     `a` toward the field, `b` toward 0.
   - A positive `b` means the car loses more in fast sectors; a negative `b`
     means it's relatively strong there.
   - At a target track, the predicted deficit is `a + b·x_track`, where
     `x_track = Σ sector share × (sector trap speed − 250) / 50`. It comes from
     that circuit's earlier dry profiles, or the field average for a new venue.
   - The same form is fitted for race pace, against each event's `x`.
3. **Driver offsets** relative to the teammate, for qualifying and race pace:
   recency-weighted and shrunk toward 0.

   **The car is shared by both drivers.** A team's qualifying sector deficit at
   an event is the *average* of its drivers' deficits (`CAR_PACE = "mean"`).
   Before 2026-09-26 it was the team's *best* sector times, which credited the
   faster driver's pace to the car and then again through that driver's offset.

   Measured over 2021–2026 (as-of predictions vs actual):

   | | Car share | Driver share |
   |---|---|---|
   | Qualifying-deficit spread | 61% | 39% |
   | Race-pace spread | 86% | 14% |

   | Teammates' surprises (actual − predicted) | Correlation |
   |---|---|
   | Qualifying | +0.49 |
   | Race pace | +0.63 |
   | Finishing position | +0.19 |
   | DNF | +0.11 |
4. **Finishing model:** a ridge regression, fit on earlier races, of normalized
   finishing position on:
   - `g`: normalized grid;
   - `rp_rel`: predicted race-pace gap;
   - `qp_rel`: predicted qualifying gap;
   - interactions with the track's **overtaking ease**
     (`mean_places_gained / 2.8`): `g_ease` and `rp_ease`;
   - `street_g`: grid × street circuit.
5. **Simulation** (10,000 runs). **Teammates share part of the luck:** the
   qualifying and finishing noise is split into a team part (one draw per car
   per simulated race) and a driver part. The teammate correlation is measured
   on races before the cutoff, about 0.5 for qualifying and 0.2 for finishing.
   Each driver's own spread is unchanged. Teammate head-to-heads become more
   decisive and team one-twos more likely (`TEAMMATE_CORR`).
   - **Grid:** the real grid if qualifying has happened, otherwise simulated from
     qualifying pace plus noise (σ estimated from history).
   - **Grid penalties:** confirmed penalties are listed per event and session in
     `sports/f1/grid_penalties.toml` (event `YYYY-RR`, driver surname or full name matched exactly after
     lowercasing and dropping accents, session `race` or `sprint`, a source, an optional `announced` date).
     Three kinds: `back` (back of the grid), `pit_lane` (priced as back of the grid) and `places` with
     `places = N` (N places back, the drivers behind closing up). `penalties.apply` moves the known or simulated
     grid after its draws (`simulate_race(grid_adjust=...)`), so no random draw is added or moved and a weekend
     without a penalty prices byte-identically. Pole is still priced from the qualifying order, and the
     applied penalties are recorded in the run's `audit.grid_penalties`. A driver name that matches no entrant
     is an error, not a no-op.
   - **Finish:** a finishing score plus noise (σ from the regression residuals).
   - **DNFs:** each team's recency-weighted DNF rate.
   - **Points:** official points (race 25-18-15-…, sprint 8-7-…, 2021 sprint 3-2-1).
   - **Championship:** current official points plus the simulated remaining races
     and sprints.
6. **Sprint stage** (sprint weekends; [U13](todo.md), provisional). Each race-like session is a row of
   `[sessions.sim]` in `sports/f1.toml`: `race = {grid_from = "qual", points = "race"}` and
   `sprint = {grid_from = "sprint_qual", points = "sprint", dnf_scale = 0.5}`. On a weekend that has a sprint,
   `pricing.price_stages` runs the same `simulate_race` a second time with the Grand Prix's paces and finishing
   model, the sprint points table (2021: 3-2-1) and each driver's DNF rate × `dnf_scale`. No sprint-specific grid
   weight or noise yet.
   - **Grid:** Sprint Qualifying's laps ranked by each driver's best valid lap once SQ has run (FastF1 stores
     SQ without positions, so the classification can't be used; segment-aware SQ1/SQ2/SQ3 ordering is still
     open). Before SQ, the grid is simulated from qualifying pace plus noise, as for the Grand Prix.
   - **Per-year grid source:** `grid_from_by_year` (`model.grid_source(stage, year)`) overrides `grid_from` for a
     season: in 2021 Friday's qualifying set the sprint grid (there was no Sprint Qualifying), so a 2021 sprint
     takes its grid from `qual`'s classification once qualifying has run (qualifying laps aren't practice rows).
   - **After the sprint:** its actual classification and points, the way the Grand Prix uses the actual
     qualifying order.
   - **As-of:** the stage reads SQ and the sprint only from the as-of view, so a stage before them never sees
     their results (`tests/test_sprint_stage.py`).
   - **Nothing else moves:** the draws come from a side stream seeded from the main one without advancing it.
     So every Grand Prix price, the constructor tie-break and the caller's next draws are byte-identical with
     or without the stage, and a weekend without a sprint prices exactly as before.
   - **Outputs:** the summary gains `sprint_win_prob` and `sprint_pole_prob` (stored in
     `race_predictions.extra`). The sims gain `stage_rank["sprint"]` and `["sprint_qual"]` plus the sprint's
     finished and points; `OutcomeSims.at("sprint")` is the sprint as a race of its own.
   - **Walk-forward:** `racinglines f1 sprint-check` (`models/position_sim/sprint_check.py`) prices every
     stored sprint a minute before SQ and a minute before the sprint. It scores the sprint winner (Brier, log
     loss) against the Grand Prix win stand-in and counts sprint and GP retirements per weekend, the evidence
     for `dnf_scale`. It has not been run on real data yet.

Team lineage is kept across renames: Racing Point → Aston Martin, Renault →
Alpine, Toro Rosso / AlphaTauri → RB, Alfa Romeo / Kick Sauber / Audi → Sauber.

## Using it

```
racinglines f1 backtest --save             # historical: every race before qualifying and before the race
racinglines f1 diagnostic --event 2026-15 --cutoff 2026-09-25T13:30 --save   # one past event as of a cutoff
racinglines f1 forecast --year 2026 --save # live: remaining races + championship
```

All three price through `position_sim.pricing.price_race` with a hard as-of cutoff. See
[Market making](market-making.md#two-separate-things).
Backtest results are on [F1 evaluation](f1-evaluation.md); the current live
forecast is on [F1 forecast](f1-forecast.md).

A saved forecast drives the web app's Markets page, race pages and quotes. Upcoming Grands
Prix become `scheduled` events with per-driver win, podium, top-10 (points) and
pole probabilities; championship standings are stored too.

The **private book** works for F1 as well. Markets use Grand Prix wording, and
there's a `race_top10` (points finish) market in place of "makes the Final".
Auto-settlement reads the `race` round and requires a classified finish.

Prix?", "Rain during the Bahrain Grand Prix?") are linked to their race as `race_safety_car`, `race_red_flag`
and `race_rain` with no model price (the live props book prices them from history, `models/position_sim/props.py`).
Any other `<GP>: ...` event (constructor fastest lap, ...) is linked to its race but stays unmodeled, and other
props are synced with their prices only.

## Weather-aware (-WX) variants

A `-WX` variant is its base model or method priced with a probability of rain for the race (`p_wet`). Which markets
the weather moves is set by the kinds schema, not by code: each kind has a `wx` (`racinglines/markets/kinds.py`,
`wx = "..."` in `markets/kinds.toml`). `wx = "rate"` (the red flag) is priced with `props.rate_wx`, the circuit's
wet-race rate times `p_wet` plus its dry-race rate times `1 - p_wet`. `wx = "dnf"` (every classification and
retirement kind: classified, retired, last classified, n-th retirement, driver and team) is priced from the run's
`dnf_prob` multiplied by `props.wx_scale`: the circuit's retirement rate at that `p_wet` over the same at its usual
wet share, so a forecast that says no more than the history leaves the price unchanged. The safety car has no `wx`
(conditioning it was worse in the walk-forward); rain is not conditioned but priced at `p_wet` itself. Without a
`p_wet` every price is the plain one. The live book passes one only for its props (rain and red flag) and only when a
wet vote is saved for the race ([Weather: wet-race forecast](weather.md#wet-race-forecast)); its retirement and
classification markets keep the run's `dnf_prob`.

The backtest takes the forecasts issued days before each past race, the leads CSV of `racinglines weather leads`
(committed: `tests/fixtures/weather/open_meteo-leads-f1.csv`, one row per race, lead and model), turned into a `p_wet`
per race by `weather/wet.py`'s `p_wet_series`: each model's vote at that lead, shrunk to the circuit's wet share as of
the race, the same vote `racinglines weather backtest` scores. `racinglines f1 props --check --history <csv> --forecast
<csv> --lead 5` adds `climatology-WX` (the red flag and safety car with `rate_wx` at the forecast's `p_wet` instead of
the circuit's wet share) and `--dnf-check <csv> --forecast <csv> --history <csv> --lead 5` adds `model-WX` (the
retirement twin of the live simulation variant, `gridq+pretrain+reset-WX`), each with a paired Brier difference against
its base (`climatology-WX - climatology`, `model-WX - model`). The forecast's own skill by lead is
`racinglines weather backtest`. Results go in the [decision log](f1-roadmap.md#decision-log) before any of it is
treated as final.

**Placeholders.** Some race events carry unnamed slots, "Driver A" to "Driver E" and "any other driver" (or
"constructor"), so Polymarket can add outcomes later without a new event. They name no driver, so we can't price
them: they stay unmodeled, linked to the race, with `params.placeholder` set to the kind they stand in for
(`race_win`, `race_pole`, ...). The race page leaves them out of each market's table and says how many there are
under it; the linked-markets admin page shows them as "placeholder". Rows synced before 2026-10-04 get the
tag on the next Polymarket sync.

**Sync** (`racinglines/markets/polymarket/sync.py`, the **Refresh** button on `/markets/polymarket`, or
`POST /pm/sync`):

- **Events:** reads every active event tagged `f1` / `formula1` from Polymarket's
  public Gamma API.
- **Classification:** each market is classified by its title.
- **Drivers:** matched by full name, unique surname or last two names, plus
  aliases such as `"Jr."` → Carlos Sainz.
- **Teams:** matched by name, including sponsor labels such as
  "Mclaren Mastercard" and "Tgr Haas".
- **Races:** matched by Grand Prix name within the season (FastF1's short event
  name is stored in `races.format.event_name`).
- **Storage:** one `market_links` row per outcome token, with live bid/ask/mid,
  volume, end date, and resolution once the market closes.

**Makers** use the **Polymarket page** (`/markets/polymarket`, linked from Markets):

- Per event: each outcome's Polymarket bid/ask/mid, our fair value, the edge (our
  fair − Polymarket mid), and suggested quotes at fair ± spread/2.
- **Mirror into my book** creates or reprices a YES/NO market in the maker's
  book for every priced outcome, linked to the Polymarket token.
- Takers then see those markets on their Markets board (`/markets`), with the live Polymarket
  price and a link next to each maker quote.
- Season-long markets (championships, props) appear under "Season-long markets".
- Admin refreshes also **settle mirrored markets from Polymarket's resolution**.

## Results

Over 129 races (2021 to Azerbaijan 2026), each priced before practice, before
qualifying and after qualifying, the model beats a uniform guess at every stage
and a grid-only guess on top 10 after qualifying. With the `gridq+pretrain+reset`
variant it also beats the grid-only guess on win and podium. Against Polymarket
it is competitive in 2026 (better mid-weekend on race winners) but the market is
sharper in 2025 and after qualifying. Full tables: [F1 evaluation](f1-evaluation.md).

### Model variants (F1 roadmap F1-2, F1-3)

The variants (`grid`, `gridq`, `pretrain`, `gbm`, `tail`, `reset`, `rookie`) and their
backtest comparisons are in
[F1 evaluation → Model variants](f1-evaluation.md#model-variants-f1-roadmap-f1-2-f1-3).
`fastlap` (provisional, uncalibrated) changes no price: it adds the race's fastest lap to the simulation
(`extra.fl_prob`, the `race_fastest_lap` kind), so it combines with any of them, e.g. `gridq+pretrain+reset+fastlap`. `flpos` (provisional, 2026-10-07) does the same
with the fastest lap drawn from the simulated finishing order and race pace (`sports/f1/fastest_lap.toml`), so the
winner sets it about as often as in real races; combo pricing (`markets/combos.py`, the `f1_combo` Lab job) uses it.
