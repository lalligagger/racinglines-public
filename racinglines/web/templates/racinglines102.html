Racinglines 102

Where the edge comes from, how a strategy picks from it, and the one kind of trade that knowingly gives a little of it back. Read Racinglines 101 first for makers and takers.

The one-sentence version

The model prices every line; a strategy only acts where the model's price and the market's price are far enough apart, always on the side the model calls cheap. So no strategy ever opens a bet the model thinks is a losing one. Once it holds a position, though, it will sometimes sell for less than the model says it's worth, on purpose, to carry less risk.

Step 1: the model finds the gaps

For every market, the prediction engine gives a fair probability. The exchange gives a price. The gap between them is the edge:

  • Model says Norris wins 45%, the market sells YES at 30¢: the edge is +15 points on YES. Buying YES is +EV (positive expected value).
  • Model says 20%, the market prices him at 30¢: the edge is on the other side. Buying NO at 70¢ for something the model rates 80% is the +EV trade.

"+EV" here always means by the model's own numbers. A gap is only real if the model is right, which is why the strategies below don't trade every gap they see.

Step 2: the strategy filters them

Takers pick lines

Strategy A buys only when the gap is at least 10 points (5 for head-to-head markets), and only in markets that are open, liquid, and whose prices across the field add up. The bigger the gap, the bigger the bet, up to $50 a market. A small gap is treated as noise in the model, not a bargain.

Makers don't pick lines

A maker posts a bid a little below the model's price and an ask a little above it (2¢ either side), and lets other traders come to it. Strategy C refuses to quote where the model and the market disagree by more than 5 points: those are exactly the lines a taker wants, and for a maker they are the ones most likely to mean the model has missed something.

One detail on costs. The taker's 10-point test uses the raw gap, before the cent or two each trade costs (spread, slippage, and Kalshi's fee). The costs are added to the price when the bet is sized. At a 10-point threshold that can't turn a trade negative, but anyone who set the threshold below the costs could. The Kelly sizing option checks the edge after costs and bets nothing when it's gone.

Step 3: after the bet, the model keeps moving

Strategy A is an update taker: after every practice, sprint and qualifying session it re-prices each market and resizes its position to fit the new gap. That gives three possible outcomes for a bet it already holds.

The new gapWhat A doesIs that +EV?
Still big, same sideKeeps the position, or tops it upYes
Shrunk, same sideSells some back. Under 10 points, it sells all of itSlightly negative. The model still rates the line a little above the price, so selling gives up a little expected value (plus the cost of the trade) in exchange for holding less
Flipped to the other sideSells the whole position, and if the new gap is 10 points or more, buys the other sideYes, even when the sale books a loss. See below

Selling at a loss isn't the same as a -EV trade

Say A bought YES at 30¢ when the model said 45%. After qualifying the model says 20%, and the market has moved to 25¢. Selling now books a 5¢ loss. But the model now values that share at 20¢, and the market will pay 25¢ for it: selling is the +EV move. What A paid for it is gone either way, and it plays no part in the decision. That is the honest answer to "can we minimize a loss on a -EV play?": when the model turns against a bet, the strategy gets out at the best price on offer, and getting out is itself the +EV trade.

When a strategy knowingly gives up edge

A few rules trade some expected value for less risk. They are deliberate, and each is named so it can be tested:

  • The taker trimming a shrinking gap (above). On by default in update mode. The stage-aware taker (mode early) stops trading before the last sessions and holds to the finish instead.
  • The maker leaning against its inventory. The more a maker holds, the more both its quotes shift to attract trades that shrink the position. At the default lean, a maker at its full position offers to sell back right at the model's price: no edge, but no loss of it either.
  • The maker leaning harder before a session (info_skew) and going flat before qualifying (flatten_before_qual). Both can sell below the model's price to shed a position before the biggest news of the weekend. Both are off by default, and off in C.

What doesn't exist

  • No stop-loss. No strategy sells because a position is down money. Strategies sell because the model changed its mind, never because the price did.
  • No -EV entries. Nothing opens a position on the side the model calls expensive.
  • The closest thing to a loss limit is the private book's max_loss (off by default): once a market's worst case reaches the cap, the maker stops adding to that side. It never sells out.

A "cut it when it's gone against us" rule could be added as a new taker setting (for example, exit when the model's fair has moved some number of points against the entry). Like every tuned setting, it would have to win a backtest sweep in both seasons before it became a default.

The caveat that sits under all of this

Every "+EV" on this page is the model's opinion. The strategies are built to trust it only where it has earned trust: big gaps for takers, small ones for makers, and a paper-trading season to see whether the backtests hold up.
